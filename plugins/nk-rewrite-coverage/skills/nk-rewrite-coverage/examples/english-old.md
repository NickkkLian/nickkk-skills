# Lumen Harbor

## Purpose

The practice team uses this invented handbook to rehearse a handover without relying on memory. The exercise begins with an ordinary folder and ends with a readable record that another person can use without asking what happened. Neri Vale keeps the shared register, while Mara Fen holds the spare key. The record describes what was checked, which copy was carried across the room, and why a later reader should be able to repeat the same small routine. The team treats clarity as a way to preserve evidence rather than a reason to remove awkward details from the page.

## Preparation

The supply shelf contains 1,250 blank cards, with 73 blue cards held separately. The trial assumes a 19% reserve so that an interrupted exercise can resume without borrowing supplies from another group. The register stays open for 24 hours after the rehearsal. The first drawer holds the **reserve envelope**, which stays apart from the working papers so that a tired reader cannot mistake it for the current batch. The instructions are available at https://example.com/harbor/guide/ and the contingency map is at https://example.org/harbor/plan. The addresses are fictional and are printed here only to make the exercise reproducible.

## Checks

Before touching the working folder, run `harborctl status --plain`. The optional diagnostic is `harborctl check --batch amber`, which leaves a local note explaining whether the spare route is available. The first packet contains 8 cards. The next packet also contains 8 cards. The final packet again contains 8 cards. The operator keeps each packet separate until the register has been checked, then places the accepted packets together. The routine uses small batches because a missing card is easier to locate before the working folder becomes crowded with unrelated paper.

## Handover

The reminder on the working folder reads “carry the amber card”. The desk label repeats “carry the amber card”. The closing note repeats “carry the amber card”. The side instruction says “leave the side gate open”. The person receiving the folder reads the reminder aloud and then checks that the paper copy agrees with the register. Bring the folder to a clear table; an empty tray and an uninterrupted minute are enough. The receiver can move the tray after the check, provided the folder and its accepted packets continue to travel together.

## Record

The note archive lives at https://example.net/harbor/notes and remains open for the same 24 hours. Record a short explanation of any interruption, including what was left on the table and whether another person took over. The explanation should be useful to someone who did not attend the exercise. The following labels distinguish the regular route from the spare route without requiring a separate legend.

| route | handling |
|---|---|
| main tray | keep beside the register |
| quiet slot | leave a separate paper note |

## Close

Check the register against the folder before filing either copy. The team then reviews whether the instructions were easy to follow while attention was divided between the table and the receiving desk. The purpose is to improve the next rehearsal through a concrete record, not to reward a neat page at the expense of what the page once told its reader. The operator leaves the folder in its labelled tray and closes the exercise only after the receiver has confirmed that the accepted packets are present.
