# Lumen Harbor

## Purpose

The practice team uses this invented handbook to rehearse a handover without relying on memory. The exercise begins with an ordinary folder and ends with a readable record that another person can use without asking what happened. Neri Vale keeps the shared register. The record describes what was checked, which copy was carried across the room, and why a later reader should be able to repeat the same small routine. The team treats clarity as a way to preserve evidence rather than a reason to remove awkward details from the page.

## Preparation

The supply shelf contains blank cards, with blue cards held separately. The trial leaves a reserve so that an interrupted exercise can resume without borrowing supplies from another group. The register stays open for 24 hours after the rehearsal. The first drawer holds the spare papers apart from the working papers so that a tired reader cannot mistake them for the current batch. The instructions are available at https://example.com/harbor/guide. The addresses are fictional and are printed here only to make the exercise reproducible.

## Checks

The operator runs `harborctl status --plain` before touching the working folder. The packets each contain 8 cards. The operator keeps each packet separate until the register has been checked, then places the accepted packets together. The routine uses small batches because a missing card is easier to locate before the working folder becomes crowded with unrelated paper.

## Handover

The reminder on the working folder reads "carry the amber card". The desk label and closing note repeat the reminder. The side instruction is omitted from the tidy copy. The person receiving the folder reads the reminder aloud and then checks that the paper copy agrees with the register. The handover needs a clear table, an empty tray, and an uninterrupted minute. The receiver can move the tray after the check, provided the folder and its accepted packets continue to travel together.

## Record

The note archive lives at https://example.net/harbor/notes and remains open for the same 24 hours. The operator writes a short explanation of any interruption, including what was left on the table and whether another person took over. The explanation should be useful to someone who did not attend the exercise. The following label identifies the regular route.

| route | handling |
|---|---|
| main tray | keep beside the register |

## Close

The final reader checks the register against the folder before filing either copy. The team reviews whether the instructions were easy to follow while attention was divided between the table and the receiving desk. The purpose is to improve the next rehearsal through a concrete record, not to reward a neat page at the expense of what the page once told its reader. The operator leaves the folder in its labelled tray and closes the exercise only after the receiver has confirmed that the accepted packets are present.
